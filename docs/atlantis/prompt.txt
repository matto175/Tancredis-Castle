RENDERING APPROACH (recommendations; deviate where you find something better, and say why)
Realism comes mostly from lighting and material response, then from detail. Because the sun, sky and most of the world never change, a lot can be computed once at load and cached. Spend that budget on quality.
1. HDR linear pipeline (rgba16float). A physically based sky and aerial perspective (for example Hillaire-style transmittance, sky-view and aerial-perspective LUTs), computed once since time of day is fixed. Sun disc and halo. Filmic tone mapping (AgX- or ACES-like) at a fixed exposure, with dithering against banding.
2. Image-based lighting from that sky: a prefiltered specular cubemap plus diffuse SH, built once at load. Optionally add a few static reflection probes in the city, so stone and glass reflect white architecture and water, not only sky.
3. Sun shadows: cascaded shadow maps, texel-snapped and stable, with soft filtering. The sun is fixed and most geometry is static, so cache the static casters and redraw only what moves (or ignore sway in distant cascades).
4. Ambient occlusion: screen-space AO (GTAO-like) for contact shadows, plus per-vertex or analytic AO baked at generation time. Grounding is the number-one cue that separates realistic from cartoonish.
5. Materials: GGX microfacet BRDF with energy-conserving diffuse and specular AA (roughness widened by normal variance). Procedural detail (triplanar marble veining, pores, plaster grain, polished vs honed zones, waterline darkening and moss) that fades with distance instead of aliasing.
6. Geometry: bevel or round every architectural edge so it catches a highlight. Add secondary detail (cornices, stepped bases, window reveals and frames, dome ribs, balustrades, planters, vines). Generate everything from a small set of parametric generators: revolved profiles, extruded or tapered n-gons, arched arcade modules, swept arcs and a heightfield terrain, all instanced.
7. Vegetation, the highest-risk area for realism: real trunk and branch structure with clustered leaf cards (alpha-to-coverage with MSAA, or dithered with TAA), backlit translucency, wind sway in the vertex shader, columnar cypress, and instanced flower clusters in beds and planters. Use a leaf budget like the underwater project's. No sphere or blob canopies.
8. Water: screen-space reflections falling back to a planar reflection or the probes and sky (canals must clearly mirror the crystal, towers and domes); refraction with depth absorption and a visible stone floor; filtered procedural wave normals; caustics on floors and on quay walls near the water. The indigo light-liquid uses the same pipeline with dark absorption, low waves and emissive flow filaments.
9. Crystal and glass: screen-space refraction of the opaque scene behind (sample the colour buffer with per-channel offsets for edge dispersion), true facet normals, internal facet reflections from the environment, fresnel, and a restrained emissive core. Domes are thin glass (reflection plus faintly tinted transmission) with ribs as real geometry.
10. Atmosphere and post: height fog and aerial perspective from the LUTs, god rays (shadow-map or screen-space, low resolution), an energy-conserving bloom chain at low intensity (no sparkle stars), a subtle vignette, no film grain and no heavy chromatic aberration.
11. Anti-aliasing: whichever gives the cleanest motion. TAA with neighbourhood clamping handles foliage and specular aliasing best; MSAA 4× plus specular AA is the fallback if TAA ghosts. Filter all thin procedural lines (inlay, ribs) analytically with screen-space derivatives.
12. Stability: no hard LOD swaps (morph or dithered cross-fade), instance density that thins with distance, reversed-Z float depth, and slow, smooth animation driven by a time uniform.

PERFORMANCE DISCIPLINE
- Generate the world on the GPU at load. Animate on the GPU (sway, water, the pulse, craft paths, clouds) from a time uniform; no per-instance CPU updates.
- One instanced draw per mesh type; GPU frustum culling or indirect draws where they help; a small number of pipelines.
- GPU timestamp queries per pass from day one, so you always know where the milliseconds go.
- When over budget, cut in this order (cut the lowest first, never cut the top tier): craft, pulse ripples on water, god rays, flower density far away, small caustics → vegetation density, reflection quality → (never cut) sky and atmosphere, tone mapping, water reflections, shadows and AO, the crystal and lotus, the ring layout.

WORKFLOW
- Depth before breadth. Build the harness first (camera, controls, shots, fly-through, timing, render-to-PNG). Then get the lighting stack (sky, IBL, shadows, AO, tone mapping) and the materials looking right on a few hero buildings, until a stripped-down P2 already reads as photoreal. Only then populate the city. A few elements that look real beat many that look like props.
- Suggested order: harness → sky, atmosphere and sea → terrain and layout → lighting stack and materials on the podium, crystal and one bridge (check P2, P4) → water (P3) → buildings and vegetation (P1, P5) → motion and remaining details (P6, fly-through).
- Mirror the underwater project's structure: readable source/ modules, a build script that emits the minified index.html, and a dev.html with the fly-through and benchmark.
- Before trusting any render or timing, confirm Chrome is using the RTX 5070 Ti hardware adapter (not SwiftShader or a software fallback).
- Never create or edit files through the shell; use the Edit/Write tools. Work token-efficiently.
- Any command running longer than 120 s is wrong or needs a faster approach; don't loop on it.
- Cap the FPS during development and never leave the scene running in the background when it isn't needed.
